Galaxies and jets as flows of space

The constant-Lagrangian metric-inflow galaxy model and the three-rapidity Painlevé–Gullstrand jet geometry: interactive models to explore, videos that show the flow, fits of the model to all 175 SPARC rotation curves, and the biquaternion algebra it is built on. The constant-Lagrangian rotation law is no longer only a fit: it follows from one gravitational rapidity rotor, along three routes that meet in the same condition.

  • Why the constant-Lagrangian law

    The rotation law fitted to SPARC follows from a single gravitational rapidity field. A radial (Newtonian plus Hubble) and an azimuthal rapidity feed one local Lorentz rotor G. Three constructions from that rotor arrive at the same condition, isorapidity: the composed Lorentz factor Γ = cosh χr cosh χφ is conserved along the flow.

    • Kinematic: the time channel of the convective four-acceleration is c DΓ/Dτ. Isorapidity is zero power along the flow, and the river is turned but never sped up.
    • Maurer–Cartan: the time channel of the rotor’s own connection, transported with the flow, is (c/2) DΓ/Dτ, exactly. The identity Ag = ΩτUg + UgΩτ† makes the two one statement.
    • Metric: in the Painlevé–Gullstrand metric of the same flow, the condition reads, to slow-flow order, as flow along levels of the redshift factor √(−gtt).

    With the Newtonian virial condition at the bulge radius R as boundary data, the conserved value gives the rotation curve vφ² = (3/2)vr(R)² − vr(r)², with vr = √(2GM/r) − Hr: no halo and no interpolation function.

  • The BQ algebra behind the models

    The galaxy and jet models are built on biquaternion (BQ) algebra: four-vectors as 2×2 complex matrices, where one product of two four-vectors gives scalar, space and spin channels at once. A separate site builds it up in eleven interactive pages and four themes, with Python scripts that check every result exactly.

    • Algebra only: the BQ-Pauli multiplication table
    • Vectors and their products: the bilinear ATB, the triple product D(ATB), field quadratics
