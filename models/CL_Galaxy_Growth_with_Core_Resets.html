CL Galaxy Growth with Core Resets

One constant-Lagrangian galaxy, grown from the earliest moment it can exist to today in a flat ΛCDM background. The CL mass M grows by a standard cosmological inflow (mass-accretion) history, H(t) and a(t) follow ΛCDM, and the critical radius rc = (2GM/H²)1/3, the radius where the radial river stalls, opens up with the expansion. Press play or drag the time slider. This page adds an exploratory foreground to the baseline model: a fixed core collects part of the inflow, a disc builds up outside it, and when the disc reaches k times the core mass the core resets. Earlier stages remain as fossil rings that merge over Nint orbits.

What the model does

a(t) = (Ωm/ΩΛ)1/3 sinh2/3(³⁄₂√ΩΛ H₀ t) , H(t) = H₀√ΩΛ coth(³⁄₂√ΩΛ H₀ t)
rc = (2GM/H²)1/3 = (2GM/H₀²)1/3 · a · (Ωm + ΩΛa³)−1/3
  • rc and a(t). For fixed M, rc ∝ a exactly while matter dominates, and freezes at (2GM/H₀²ΩΛ)1/3 once Λ takes over. With mass growth, rc ∝ a·M1/3 in the matter era.
  • rc as a density radius. The mean density inside rc is 3M/4πrc³ = 3H²/8πG, exactly the critical density. The ΛCDM virial radius R200 is the radius of 200 times that density, so R200 = rc/2001/3 ≈ rc/5.85 for the same mass.
  • The CL curve at each epoch. X = √(2GM/R) − HR, vL² = ³⁄₂X²; v² = ½X²r²/R² inside R and vL² − (√(2GM/r) − Hr)² outside, with the epoch's own M, R and H. The spiral streamlines follow tan α = vr,eff/vφ and wind up into the circle rc; the view draws them from 0.7 rc inward, because closer to rc they wind so tightly that the turns merge.
  • Core + disc with resets, sized by the embedding condition. The core radius follows your size law GMcore/R² = ε·cH(t) (de Haas 2025c): it is set at onset and again at each reset, with the epoch's own H(t), and stays fixed in between. Inflowing mass is split: a share 1 − fd crosses R and joins the core mass Mcore, which alone drives the CL flow; the share fd stays outside R as a disc of test masses. When the disc reaches k·Mcore it is no longer a test mass: the disc joins the core (all mass inside the new R, M2 = everything), the spiral of the ending stage freezes as a fossil between the old and new radius, and the new radius follows from the embedding condition with the new mass. No ΛCDM size relation enters this layer. Because R ∝ (M/H)1/2, the plateau obeys vL⁴ ≈ 9εGcH·Mcore: a baryonic Tully–Fisher slope of 4 at every epoch, with a zero point that scales with H(t). ε = 0.02 reproduces the observed BTFR (Mbar = 47 M☉ (km/s)−4 V⁴, Vflat ≈ vL/1.065) for M₀ taken as the total baryonic mass, and leaves today's core at GM/R² ≈ 10−1.2 cH₀, close to the 10−1.0 found in the SPARC CL fits. The disc is drawn out to 3.5 R, the SPARC median ratio of the optical edge to R.
  • Integration of earlier stages. Each fossil stage counts the orbits N completed inside the core since its reset (solid-body Ω = v(R)/R of the active core). Its ring and spiral fade as w = exp(−N/Nint), and inside R the rotation curve shifts with the same weight from the frozen curve of the stage that built each band (L₁ remnants) to the solid-body interior of the current Lagrangian: v² = w·vstage² + (1 − w)·vnow². The pink solid line is this blend; the dashed line is the fully merged interior. A stage counts as still distinguishable while w > 0.1, and the readout calls the galaxy nested or single-L accordingly. Inner rings complete hundreds to thousands of orbits and merge; only a recent reset stays visible, so the model predicts that galaxies with clean two-L fits had their last reset late.
  • Fossil orientation. A frozen spiral keeps the pattern angle it had at its reset, accumulated as ∫Ω dt over the core's history. After many orbits that angle is effectively random; the alternative setting draws a seeded random angle per reset. Neither carries information, so consecutive fossils do not line up into a spiral of their own.
  • Few resets. Resets need fd > k/(1+k), and each one raises the core mass by M₂/M₁ = 1 + k/(fd − k(1 − fd)). Two to four resets since tstart, with jumps of ×5–20, come from fd about 0.03–0.15 above the threshold: k = 1.5 with fd 0.65–0.75, k = 2 with fd 0.70–0.85, k = 3 with fd 0.80–0.90, k = 4 with fd 0.85–0.95. Far above the threshold resets become frequent and approach continuous growth R(t) = (GM/εcH)1/2; below it there is no reset and the disc grows without bound. The sliders are limited to this neighbourhood, and the regime readout shows the jump and its class.
  • Optical disc and dark-matter edges (switch, off by default). The halo mass today follows from the plateau speed, V200 = vL/(Vmax/V200) and M200 = V200³/(10GH₀), and grows with the same accretion history. The dark-matter edge is RΔ = (2GMh/ΔH²)1/3, with Δvir(z) from Bryan & Norman (1998) or Δ = 200. The optical edge is Ropt = 3.2 Rd, with Rd from r1/2 = 0.015 R200c, so Ropt ≈ 0.029 R200c. Note that rc has the same form with Δ = 1 and the CL mass M in place of the halo mass.
  • Earliest possible onset. The galaxy starts at the latest of three conditions: M(t) ≥ the minimum galaxy mass, R(t) < rc(t) (otherwise X ≤ 0 and no CL structure exists), and t ≥ tstart, the chosen start time (default 0.4 Gyr, z ≈ 11).

What is assumed

  • imposed Flat ΛCDM with Ωm = 0.31, H₀ = 67.9 km/s/Mpc (= 2.2×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹, the value used in the SPARC CL fits); radiation neglected, so the earliest ~1 Myr are not exact.
  • calibrated The growth laws are halo mass-accretion histories from ΛCDM simulations (McBride et al. 2009; Wechsler et al. 2002) or the power law of de Haas (2026a). Applying them to the CL mass M, which tracks the inner (bulge-like) mass rather than the halo, is an assumption of this model.
  • calibrated Average size relations: Ropt = 3.2 Rd (Persic & Salucci 1991), r1/2 ≈ 0.015 R200c (Kravtsov 2013), Δvir(z) (Bryan & Norman 1998), Vmax/V200 ≈ 1.2–1.4 for Milky-Way-mass haloes. These are population averages with about 0.2 dex scatter, applied here to one galaxy. Both edges are the standard dark-matter picture, drawn for comparison with rc; the CL model itself has no halo.
  • calibrated Four parameters of the core + disc layer: the disc capture fraction fd (0.75), the reset threshold k (2), the embedding factor ε (0.02, from the BTFR zero point) and the integration time Nint (30 orbits, a plausible range is 20–100; not calibrated). The size law itself, GM/R² = ε·cH(t), is your embedding condition; only its factor is calibrated.
  • assumed The core radius R follows one of three simple histories; none is derived from the CL theory yet.
  • derived rc(t), vL(t), the rotation curve and the spiral at each epoch follow from M(t), R(t) and H(t) with no further freedom.
  • not included Bars, mergers, virial zones and the self-gravity of the disc. The core + disc layer is a one-way kinematic foreground on the clean CL background, and the angular momentum that merging fossil stages must lose is not tracked.
